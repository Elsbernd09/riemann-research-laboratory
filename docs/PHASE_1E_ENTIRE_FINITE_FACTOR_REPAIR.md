# Phase 1E Entire Finite-Factor Repair

## Previous weakness

The signed orbit-splitting response used the formal geometry

\[
2P_\gamma
\to
Q_{\delta,\gamma}.
\]

Its finite factor ratio required negative multiplicities at the doubled critical-line pair.

The corresponding factor was rational.

This prevented the project from interpreting the local response through two entire finite zero factors.

---

# Repair

The project now compares two four-zero configurations:

\[
H_{\delta,\gamma},
\]

representing horizontal symmetric spreading, and

\[
V_{\delta,\gamma},
\]

representing vertical critical-line spreading.

Both configurations:

- contain four points counted with multiplicity;
- are conjugation invariant;
- are reflection invariant;
- define degree-four finite polynomial zero factors.

The project susceptibility is recovered from the matched directional contrast

\[
\chi_f(\gamma)
=
\lim_{\delta\to0}
\frac{
\sum_{H_{\delta,\gamma}}f
-
\sum_{V_{\delta,\gamma}}f
}{
2\delta^2
}.
\]

---

# Interpretation change

The preferred interpretation of

\[
\chi_f(\gamma)
\]

is no longer merely:

"response to splitting a doubled critical-line orbit horizontally."

The stronger interpretation is:

> infinitesimal horizontal-versus-vertical analytic zero-geometry contrast at a critical-line conjugate pair.

For the Li kernel, this contrast can also be expressed as the difference between Li-type derivative functionals of two degree-four reflection-invariant finite polynomial factors.

---

# Research limitation

This repair acts at the finite-factor level.

It does not construct an arithmetic deformation of the actual Riemann xi-function.

The project must continue to distinguish:

1. finite entire zero geometry;
2. xi-like entire-function structure;
3. arithmetic zeta/L-function structure.

These are not interchangeable.
