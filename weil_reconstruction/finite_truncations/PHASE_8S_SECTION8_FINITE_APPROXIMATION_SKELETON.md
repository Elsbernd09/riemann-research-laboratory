# 1. Source

Enrico Bombieri, **Remarks on Weil's quadratic functional in the theory of prime numbers, I**, Section 8.

Section title:

"The eigenvalues of finite approximations."

---

# 2. Source-extracted setup

Bombieri considers the case in which the set of points \(\rho\) is finite and has the same symmetries as the set of zeros of the Riemann zeta function.

Let

\[
Z
\]

be a finite multiset of complex numbers.

The source assumes:

\[
\rho\in Z
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\overline{\rho}\in Z
\quad\text{and}\quad
1-\rho\in Z
\]

with the same multiplicity.

For each

\[
\rho\in Z,
\]

Bombieri writes

\[
\rho=\frac12+i\gamma.
\]

The corresponding finite multiset of \(\gamma\)-values is denoted

\[
\Gamma.
\]

---

# 3. Interval cutoff

Bombieri denotes by

\[
\Phi(x)
\]

the characteristic function of the closed interval

\[
[-t,t].
\]

Thus

\[
\Phi(x)=1
\quad\text{for}\quad
x\in[-t,t],
\]

and

\[
\Phi(x)=0
\quad\text{outside that interval}.
\]

---

# 4. Finite-dimensional space

Bombieri defines a complex vector space \(V\) generated by linear combinations of the functions

\[
\Phi(x)e^{x/2},
\]

\[
\Phi(x)e^{-x/2},
\]

and

\[
\Phi(x)e^{-i\gamma x}
\quad
(\gamma\in\Gamma).
\]

Thus the Section 8 finite approximation space is not arbitrary.

It is tied directly to:

1. the two endpoint/pole-type functions \(e^{x/2}\) and \(e^{-x/2}\);
2. the finite zero-frequency set \(\Gamma\);
3. the support interval \([-t,t]\).

---

# 5. Boundary subspace

Bombieri also defines a subspace

\[
V^\circ\subset V
\]

of codimension \(2\), consisting of functions \(F(x)\in V\) satisfying

\[
F(-t)=F(t)=0.
\]

This boundary condition is source-extracted and must be preserved in any later finite matrix implementation.

---

# 6. Inner product

Bombieri uses the usual \(L^2(\mathbb R)\) inner product

\[
(P,Q)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P(x)\overline{Q(x)}\,dx.
\]

This is the finite approximation inner product context.

---

# 7. Source-extracted linear functional/operator

Bombieri defines a linear functional/operator on \(V\) by

\[
L[F](x)
=
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
F(x+u)e^{i\gamma u}\,du.
\]

The sum is over the multiset \(\Gamma\), so multiplicities are counted.

This is the first source-extracted Section 8 finite approximation object.

---

# 8. Reality lemma

Bombieri records:

If

\[
F\in V,
\]

then

\[
(L[F],F)
\]

is real.

This is important because it supports the later eigenvalue analysis.
