# Originality Discipline

## Rule 1

Do not call the result original until:

1. the theorem is fully stated;
2. the proof is complete;
3. nearby known perturbation theorems are cited;
4. nearby known Bombieri/Weil literature is checked;
5. an expert has had a chance to comment.

## Rule 2

The likely originality is not basic Weyl perturbation theory.

The possible originality is the specific packaging:

1. finite Bombieri-style matrix class;
2. explicit computational stability certificate;
3. negative-eigenvalue-count preservation;
4. reproducible RH-adjacent finite-matrix audit framework.

## Rule 3

If the theorem reduces completely to a standard textbook perturbation theorem with no meaningful specialization, then it is an expository result, not original research.

## Rule 4

If the theorem gives a clean, useful, implementation-ready stability certificate for finite Bombieri-style matrices, then it may be suitable for a professor-readable research note.
