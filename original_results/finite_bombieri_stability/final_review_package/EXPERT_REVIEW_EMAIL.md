I am working on a short finite-dimensional research note titled:

"Support-Stability Certificates for Negative Eigenvalue Counts in Finite Bombieri-Style Matrix Models."

The note does not claim progress on the Riemann Hypothesis.

The main theorem is a finite support-perturbation stability certificate. For a normalized finite matrix

\[
M(\Gamma,t)=D^{1/2}A(\Gamma,t)D^{1/2},
\]

where

\[
A(\Gamma,t)_{jk}=H_t(\alpha_j,\alpha_k),
\]

if support points satisfy

\[
|\widetilde\alpha_j-\alpha_j|\leq\delta
\]

and the kernel is locally Lipschitz with constant \(L_t(U)\), then the certificate

\[
2Nm_{\max}L_t(U)\delta<g_0(M(\Gamma,t))
\]

implies

\[
n_-(M(\widetilde\Gamma,t))=n_-(M(\Gamma,t)).
\]

I understand that the underlying perturbation tools are standard. I am trying to determine whether the Bombieri-style finite-kernel framing is useful as a computational audit note, or whether the result is fully subsumed by standard perturbation theory.

I would be grateful for feedback on:

1. correctness of the theorem and proof;
2. whether the framing is appropriate;
3. whether this is purely expository;
4. what prior literature I should cite;
5. what would make the result more mathematically meaningful.
