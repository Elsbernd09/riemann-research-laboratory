# Phase 12Q — Compact Support Arithmetic Tail Theorem

## Purpose

Phase 12P showed that compact support can make the explicit-formula prime sum finite.

Phase 12Q strengthens this into a finite-test-class theorem with an explicit support cutoff.

---

# 1. Finite test class with common support

Let

\[
\phi_j(x)=\psi_j(\log x),
\qquad
\psi_j\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)
\]

for

\[
1\le j\le m.
\]

Assume there exists

\[
B>0
\]

such that

\[
\operatorname{supp}(\psi_j)\subset[-B,B]
\]

for all \(j\).

Then

\[
\operatorname{supp}(\phi_j)\subset[e^{-B},e^B].
\]

Define

\[
a=e^{-B},\qquad b=e^B.
\]

Thus every basis function is supported in

\[
[a,b].
\]

---

# 2. Finite test functions

Let

\[
\mathcal F_R^{(m)}
=
\left\{
g=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_j\phi_j:\|a\|_2\le R
\right\}.
\]

Since every \(\phi_j\) is supported in \([a,b]\), every

\[
g\in\mathcal F_R^{(m)}
\]

is also supported in

\[
[a,b].
\]

---

# 3. Star support

Recall

\[
g^*(x)=x^{-1}\overline{g(1/x)}.
\]

If

\[
\operatorname{supp}(g)\subset[a,b],
\]

then \(g^*(x)\neq0\) only if

\[
1/x\in[a,b].
\]

Thus

\[
x\in[1/b,1/a].
\]

Therefore,

\[
\operatorname{supp}(g^*)\subset[1/b,1/a].
\]

---

# 4. Convolution support

Let

\[
F_g=g*g^*
\]

where

\[
F_g(x)=\int_0^\infty g(y)g^*(x/y)\frac{dy}{y}.
\]

For a nonzero integrand, one needs:

\[
y\in[a,b]
\]

and

\[
x/y\in[1/b,1/a].
\]

Therefore,

\[
x\in y[1/b,1/a].
\]

As \(y\in[a,b]\), it follows that

\[
x\in[a/b,b/a].
\]

Since

\[
a=e^{-B},\qquad b=e^B,
\]

we get

\[
a/b=e^{-2B},
\qquad
b/a=e^{2B}.
\]

Thus

\[
\operatorname{supp}(F_g)\subset[e^{-2B},e^{2B}].
\]

---

# Theorem 12Q.1 — Uniform support bound for \(F_g\)

Let

\[
\mathcal F_R^{(m)}
\]

be generated by basis functions

\[
\phi_j(x)=\psi_j(\log x)
\]

with

\[
\operatorname{supp}(\psi_j)\subset[-B,B]
\]

for all \(j\).

Then for every

\[
g\in\mathcal F_R^{(m)},
\]

\[
\operatorname{supp}(F_g)\subset[e^{-2B},e^{2B}].
\]

## Proof

Every \(g\in\mathcal F_R^{(m)}\) is supported in \([e^{-B},e^B]\).

By the star-support calculation,

\[
g^*
\]

is supported in \([e^{-B},e^B]\) as well, since the reciprocal of \([e^{-B},e^B]\) is the same interval.

For

\[
F_g=g*g^*,
\]

a nonzero contribution requires

\[
x=yz
\]

with

\[
y\in[e^{-B},e^B],
\qquad
z\in[e^{-B},e^B].
\]

Therefore

\[
x\in[e^{-2B},e^{2B}].
\]

\[
\square
\]

---

# 5. Star of \(F_g\)

The star operation on \(F_g\) is

\[
F_g^*(x)=x^{-1}\overline{F_g(1/x)}.
\]

Since

\[
\operatorname{supp}(F_g)\subset[e^{-2B},e^{2B}]
\]

and this interval is symmetric under reciprocal, we also have

\[
\operatorname{supp}(F_g^*)\subset[e^{-2B},e^{2B}].
\]

---

# Theorem 12Q.2 — Uniform support bound for \(F_g^*\)

Under the hypotheses of Theorem 12Q.1,

\[
\operatorname{supp}(F_g^*)\subset[e^{-2B},e^{2B}]
\]

for every

\[
g\in\mathcal F_R^{(m)}.
\]

## Proof

The interval

\[
[e^{-2B},e^{2B}]
\]

is invariant under

\[
x\mapsto1/x.
\]

Since

\[
F_g^*(x)=x^{-1}\overline{F_g(1/x)},
\]

\(F_g^*(x)\neq0\) only when

\[
1/x\in[e^{-2B},e^{2B}],
\]

which is equivalent to

\[
x\in[e^{-2B},e^{2B}].
\]

\[
\square
\]

---

# 6. Prime-sum support cutoff

The prime term in the extracted arithmetic formula is

\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Lambda(n)\{F_g(n)+F_g^*(n)\}.
\]

By Theorems 12Q.1 and 12Q.2,

\[
F_g(n)=0
\]

and

\[
F_g^*(n)=0
\]

for all integers

\[
n>e^{2B}.
\]

Therefore the prime sum has no terms past

\[
X_B=e^{2B}.
\]

---

# Theorem 12Q.3 — Prime tail vanishes above the support cutoff

Let

\[
X_B=e^{2B}.
\]

For every

\[
g\in\mathcal F_R^{(m)},
\]

the prime tail

\[
P_{>X_B}[F_g]
=
\sum_{n>X_B}\Lambda(n)\{F_g(n)+F_g^*(n)\}
\]

vanishes exactly:

\[
P_{>X_B}[F_g]=0.
\]

## Proof

If

\[
n>X_B=e^{2B},
\]

then by Theorems 12Q.1 and 12Q.2,

\[
F_g(n)=F_g^*(n)=0.
\]

Therefore every summand is zero:

\[
\Lambda(n)\{F_g(n)+F_g^*(n)\}=0.
\]

Hence

\[
P_{>X_B}[F_g]=0.
\]

\[
\square
\]

---

# Corollary 12Q.4 — Finite prime sum for the full finite test class

For every

\[
g\in\mathcal F_R^{(m)},
\]

\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Lambda(n)\{F_g(n)+F_g^*(n)\}
=
\sum_{1\le n\le e^{2B}}\Lambda(n)\{F_g(n)+F_g^*(n)\}.
\]

Thus the prime part of the arithmetic certificate is exactly finite on the entire finite test class.

---

# 7. Matrix-entry version

For basis entries, define

\[
F_{ij}=\phi_i*\phi_j^*.
\]

Since both basis functions are supported in

\[
[e^{-B},e^B],
\]

the same support argument gives

\[
\operatorname{supp}(F_{ij})\subset[e^{-2B},e^{2B}].
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Lambda(n)\{F_{ij}(n)+F_{ij}^*(n)\}
\]

is finite and has no terms beyond

\[
e^{2B}.
\]

---

# Theorem 12Q.5 — Matrix prime entries are finite

For every basis pair \(i,j\), the arithmetic prime entry

\[
P[F_{ij}]
=
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Lambda(n)\{F_{ij}(n)+F_{ij}^*(n)\}
\]

equals

\[
P[F_{ij}]
=
\sum_{1\le n\le e^{2B}}\Lambda(n)\{F_{ij}(n)+F_{ij}^*(n)\}.
\]

Thus every prime contribution to the arithmetic matrix is finite.

---

Phase 12Q proves a real arithmetic simplification:

\boxed{

}
\]

This eliminates one major arithmetic-tail problem.
