# Horizontal Defect Program

## Mission

Develop a rigorous RH-adjacent research program focused on horizontal zero displacement.

The central quantity is

\[
\beta-\frac12.
\]

A method that cannot see this quantity cannot prove RH.

---

## Guiding rule

No scalar zero-counting-only theorem can be called RH-progress.

A result can be considered RH-adjacent only if it interacts with horizontal zero location, Weil positivity, Li positivity, or explicit-formula arithmetic in a nontrivial way.

---

## Program goals

### Goal 1

Separate horizontally blind objects from horizontally sensitive objects.

### Goal 2

Identify which horizontally sensitive objects are destroyed by cancellation.

### Goal 3

Identify which horizontally sensitive objects can be made positive.

### Goal 4

Identify which positive objects are explicit-formula compatible.

### Goal 5

Build finite computational testbeds for synthetic off-critical zero configurations.

---

## First theorem target

The first theorem target is an obstruction theorem.

Working title:

**Linear Li-filter horizontal-defect obstruction**

Potential statement:

Finite real linear combinations of Li horizontal contrasts cannot produce a uniformly positive detector of horizontal displacement over all phase angles unless the detector is trivial or unless nonlinear/quadratic information is introduced.

This would not prove RH.

It would clarify why naive linear Li-filter attacks fail.

---

## Second theorem target

The second theorem target is constructive.

Working title:

**Quadratic Li-coordinate defect energy**

Potential statement:

A nonlinear energy based on

\[
\log^2 |1-1/\rho|
\]

faithfully detects single-quartet horizontal displacement.

The hard question is whether aggregation over all zeros can be connected to an arithmetic-side formula.

---

## Third theorem target

The third theorem target is classical-interface work.

Working title:

**Finite-rank Weil negativity detection**

Potential statement:

Given a synthetic off-critical zero quartet, construct finite-dimensional Weil test subspaces that detect the induced negative direction.

This would be a computational and theoretical bridge to classical Weil positivity.
