# Candidate Objects for Horizontal Defect Detection

## 1. Direct beta defect

\[
D_1(\rho)=\left(\beta-\frac12\right)^2.
\]

Strength:

Perfectly detects RH failure.

Weakness:

Not directly arithmetic-side or explicit-formula compatible.

Status:

Definitional object, not yet useful.

---

## 2. Li-coordinate radial defect

Define

\[
w(\rho)=1-\frac1\rho.
\]

Then

\[
|w(\rho)|=1
\]

if and only if

\[
\Re(\rho)=\frac12.
\]

Candidate defect:

\[
D_2(\rho)=\log^2 |w(\rho)|.
\]

Strength:

Natural in Li-coordinate geometry.

Weakness:

Nonlinear and not immediately explicit-formula compatible.

Status:

Promising diagnostic.

---

## 3. Li horizontal contrast

For a quartet perturbation, Li-type contrasts produce terms involving

\[
\cos(n\theta)\cosh(nu).
\]

The horizontal defect appears through

\[
\cosh(nu)-1.
\]

Strength:

Directly sees horizontal displacement.

Weakness:

The factor \(\cos(n\theta)\) creates sign oscillation and cancellation.

Status:

Useful for obstruction theorems.

---

## 4. Squared Li horizontal contrast

Candidate:

\[
D_4(\rho)
=
\left[
\cos(n\theta)(\cosh(nu)-1)
\right]^2.
\]

Strength:

Nonnegative.

Weakness:

Squaring may destroy direct explicit-formula compatibility and creates cross terms under aggregation.

Status:

Candidate for computational experiments.

---

## 5. Weil quadratic form

The classical Weil criterion provides a positivity framework equivalent to RH.

Strength:

Already RH-equivalent and explicit-formula compatible.

Weakness:

Hard to improve without rediscovering known theory.

Status:

Classical anchor, not a novelty claim.

---

## 6. Finite-rank Weil truncations

Finite-dimensional approximations to Weil positivity may reveal how off-critical zeros generate negative directions.

Strength:

Computationally testable.

Weakness:

Need convergence theorem or quantitative detection theorem.

Status:

Strong candidate for future work.
